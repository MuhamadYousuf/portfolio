FEATURE — THE NOISE ISSUE
TURN
IT UP

Fourteen artists, three cities and one rule: if the neighbours aren’t complaining, it isn’t finished.

The rooftop studio sits four floors above a tyre shop. By nine at night the street is still loud, so the band plays louder. Nobody planned it that way; the room simply decided.

Most of the gear is borrowed. The speakers came from a wedding hall that closed, the desk from a radio station that went digital. What is new is the attitude: record everything, keep the mistakes, release on Friday.

“People tell us to clean it up,” says the drummer, laughing over the generator. “But the noise is the point. It sounds like where we live.”

Across the city, the same idea is spreading — small rooms, cheap mics and a stubborn refusal to sound polite.
